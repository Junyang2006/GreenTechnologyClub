About us

Who we are and what we are trying to build

The Green Technology Club is a student-led proposal for a campus club focused on sustainable technology. This page explains what we stand for, who we are for, and what members actually do.

Our background

Started because the topic kept coming up

Sustainability appears in almost every course now, but usually as a single slide near the end of a lecture. Students hear the terms — carbon footprint, renewable capacity, smart grid — without ever seeing how the technology behind them works.

The Green Technology Club exists to close that gap. It is a place where students take one technology at a time, study it properly, and then explain it to each other in plain language. Everything published on this website comes out of those sessions.

The club is open to every degree programme. Some members come from technical courses, others from business or communication. That mix is deliberate: green technology is not only an engineering problem.

Open to everyone

No technical background required. Curiosity and a willingness to turn up are the only entry requirements.

Our mission

To give students a practical, honest understanding of green technology by studying it together, building small projects, and sharing what we learn with the wider campus community.

Our vision

A campus where sustainable technology is something students understand and question, not just something they are told to support — and where every graduate leaves able to explain at least one green technology properly.

Objectives

What the club sets out to do

Four objectives guide how we plan sessions, choose projects and measure whether a semester went well.

Build knowledge

Run regular sessions that explain one green technology clearly and accurately.

Apply it

Turn each topic into a small project, demonstration or measurement students can try.

Reach the campus

Hold open events so the benefit is not limited to club members only.

Change habits

Support practical campus initiatives such as e-waste collection and energy saving.

The bigger picture

Why green technology matters

Malaysia sits in a region where the effects of a warming climate are already visible — heavier rainfall, longer dry spells, and haze episodes that close schools. Green technology is the part of the response that students can realistically take part in.

Energy demand keeps rising

More devices, more cooling, more data centres. Renewable capacity has to grow faster than demand, not just grow.

Transport is changing now

Electric vehicles are moving from novelty to normal. Knowing how they work is becoming a basic literacy, not a specialism.

Cities generate the data

Sensors already measure air quality, traffic and energy use. The skill now is reading that data and acting on it.

Employers are looking for it

Sustainability roles are appearing across engineering, business and IT. Early exposure is a real advantage.

Business and communication students

The people who will eventually have to fund, sell or explain these technologies to everyone else.

Activities

What a semester looks like

Select an activity to see how it runs and what taking part involves.

One member prepares a short talk on a single technology — how it works, what it costs, where it is used. The rest of the session is questions and discussion. Slides are shared afterwards so members who missed it can still follow along.

Small teams build something testable: a solar-charged sensor, an energy monitor for a study room, or a data dashboard. Projects are deliberately small enough to finish within a semester.

Open activities such as e-waste collection points, recycling drives and energy-saving weeks. These are run with other student bodies so they reach beyond club members.

Larger events including seminars, an electric vehicle exhibition and a sustainable technology challenge. These are listed on the events page with registration open to all students.
